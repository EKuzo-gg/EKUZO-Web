## The study checklist and "Where I stop"

Do I understand what the study does and does not say about kids: yes. "About kids. Not in this study." is unmissable. The one children's study mentioned is given with both halves, "somewhat better inhibition and working memory alongside higher attention-problem and depression scores", "differences "very small"", and then "Anyone quoting the first half without the second is doing the 13.7 thing again." That is the opposite of overclaiming.

"Not my specialty. My read, checked against the paper." raises trust. So does "Three of five authors work for Creyos, whose tests were used; disclosed".

Overclaimed: nothing in this section. Nothing here says gaming makes kids smarter.

Where I got lost:

Finding C1. COMPREHENSION, SHOULD FIX. The "Dubious." bullet. "Do the math on the public data and it depends on the age you compare to: about 15 years at the sample's average age of 55, about 12 at 70, and over 20 at 40, where the study runs out of data." After a hero image that says "not in the paper", I expected to be told the number is wrong. Instead I am told it could be 12, 15 or over 20. So is the 13.7 exaggerated, or about right, or too small? I honestly cannot tell what I am supposed to conclude. One plain sentence would fix it, something like saying the gap is real in adults but there is no single honest number of years.

Finding C2. COMPREHENSION, NOTE. "True, and why I take it seriously." ends with "Hard to convince." Who is hard to convince? I think it means the lead scientist is a known skeptic of brain training, so a positive finding from him counts for more. It took me two reads.

Finding C3. COMPREHENSION, NOTE. "Cross-sectional and self-reported" is jargon, but the words after the colon explain it ("it can't say whether gaming builds the skill or skilled people game more"), so I got there. "retracted and replaced in 2023, numbers intact" made me pause: a retracted study sounds like a bad study. It is explained just enough.

Finding C4. TRUST, NOTE. "That's the half I built around: kids in my sessions do pushups and drink water before ranked." A product mention inside the fact check. Mild, and it is tied to a real finding, but it is the one place the checklist stops being a checklist.

"Where I stop" is the paragraph that made me trust him most. "Time in a game displaces something, and this study measured neither sleep nor what the gaming replaced. A small share of heavy players get into real trouble." And: "A post that makes you feel better about the hours is not a reason to stop counting them." A company that wanted my kid gaming more would not write that.

Finding C5. COMPREHENSION, SHOULD FIX. The summary sentence trips: "a thousand strangers handed a study about hours spent three days talking about which game and who's around him while he plays." I read "a study about hours spent" as one phrase and had to start over. It is the conclusion of the article, so it should read first time. Also "who's around him" has no "him" nearby to refer to.

The closing links ("one mom's account of banning it and what came next", "here's why I coach League", "my coaches answer parent questions") are offered, not pushed.
