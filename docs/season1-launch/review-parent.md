# Parent review: Season 01 ad landing page and site popup

Reviewer: Parent (audience reviewer, Build Loop). Rendered screenshots only.
Date: 2026-10-10

Who I am for this review: a US parent of an 11 year old who games most evenings, mostly Fortnite and Roblox, sometimes Minecraft. I scrolled past an EKUZO ad on Instagram, it made me smile, I tapped it. I have about 20 seconds of patience and one worry I carry everywhere: who is my kid talking to online.

Screenshots reviewed (all in `screenshots/`): `landing-d1-375-empty-fontfix.png`, `landing-d3-375-empty-fontfix.png`, `landing-d10-375-empty-fontfix.png`, `landing-d4-375-empty-fontfix.png`, `landing-d3-375-confirm.png`, `landing-d3-1440-empty-fontfix.png`, `landing-d3-1440-confirm.png`, `popup-375-empty.png`, `popup-375-confirm.png`, `popup-1440-empty.png`, plus the ad contact sheet. Where an answer sits behind a collapsed question, I read what I would see after tapping it.

## 1. The 5 second read

- **What is this?** Clear. An online esports team for kids with a real coach, practicing weekly from home. "A real team, online, from home" lands. Good.
- **Who is it for?** Half clear. It is for my kid, the gamer, and I am the one signing up ("For parents"). But I cannot tell what age. Nothing says 10 to 13 or any number. The d3 photo shows teenagers in hoodies; the popup and d10 show a kid my son's age. I genuinely do not know if he is too young.
- **What does it cost me?** Only half answered. Joining the list is free. What the season costs later is never hinted. "Do I pay anything now? No." makes me think "so how much later?"
- **What happens if I give my email?** "You'll hear first when sign-ups open." Fine, low stakes, and "we only email about EKUZO, unsubscribe anytime" helps. I do not know how often they will email me or when sign-ups open beyond "2027".

Net: I understand it in 5 seconds and the ask is small, so I lean toward typing my email. The problems show up in the next 20 seconds, when I scroll.

## 2. Does the page keep the ad's promise?

Look and headline: yes, every variant I checked. No visual bait and switch.

- **d1 "They game too much. With a coach."** The landing hero is the ad, same block world art, same red strike through, same lime. It feels like I tapped into the ad. The "cross it off" cards (Alone in their room, Just playing, "Five more minutes") are the best part of any page I saw: they name my evenings exactly.
- **d3 "They play to win. Give them a team."** Same teal photo and wording. Continuity is good. The kids in the photo read 14 to 16 to me, which feeds the age question.
- **d10 "Be proud of their screen time."** Same couch kid, same blue. The three skill cards (problem solving, teamwork, talking it through) are the most convincing thing I saw as a parent because they tell me what the coach actually works on.
- **d4 "Every kid needs a team."** Same purple card and jersey photo. Continuity is good but this page is the thinnest: one short paragraph, then the generic how it works. Nothing that reassures me beyond the ad.
- **Popup on ekuzo.gg.** Uses the d1 headline on the d10 couch photo. Reads as the same campaign. Clean, short, fine.

Substance: here is where it slips. Several ads and the pages say "the game they already love" (d5 ad), "same game they play every night" (d8 ad), "doing something they love" (d10). Then the page tells me "They play League of Legends." My kid does not play League of Legends. That is the one moment that felt like a switch (see PX-2). The d1 ad's blocky art also made me think Minecraft.

## 3. Trust: would I give my email?

Honestly, yes on d1 and d10, probably on d3, maybe on d4, because it is free, it is only an email, and the copy is warm and specific. But I would give it with my guard up, and these are the things that kept it up: no age, no word on safety or strangers, a game I have seen warnings about, nobody behind the brand I can see, and a start date over a year away. The full list is in the findings.

## 4. After sign-up

`landing-d3-375-confirm.png`, `landing-d3-1440-confirm.png`, `popup-375-confirm.png`.

"Awesome. We'll be in touch." I know I am on the list. I do not know when I will hear from them, whether a confirmation email is coming right now, or who it will be from (so I can find it and keep it out of spam). Then I am asked "what do they play?" with Fortnite and Roblox as options, right after being told they play League of Legends. That makes me wonder whether my answer decides if my kid is allowed in. See PX-6 and PX-7.

## 5. The one change, and what would make me close the tab

**Single change that would most increase my willingness:** one short, plain "is this right and safe for my kid" block right under the first email box: the ages, that it is a small private team of the same kids every week with no strangers and no public matchmaking, that a vetted adult coach runs every minute, and how the game question works. Three or four lines. That one block answers the questions I actually have before I trust anyone with my kid online.

**What would make me close the tab:** tapping "What game do they play?", reading League of Legends after the page told me "the game they already love", then searching "is League of Legends ok for an 11 year old" and finding the Teen rating and toxic chat warnings, with nothing on this page telling me why their version is different.

## Findings

### PX-1 MAJOR: No age anywhere, and the photos disagree
Screenshots: all landing variants, `popup-375-empty.png`.
Nothing on any page says who the program is for by age. d3's photo shows older teens; d10 and the popup show a kid around 10; d4 shows teens with an adult. As the parent of an 11 year old I cannot tell if this is for him or for high schoolers. A parent of a 10 year old will assume "too old" from the d3 photo and leave without signing up.
What would fix it for me: say the ages plainly near the top, once, in the same place on every variant.

### PX-2 MAJOR: "The game they already love" becomes League of Legends
Screenshots: `landing-d1-375-empty-fontfix.png` (body copy "around the game they already love" sits above the card "They play League of Legends"), `landing-d10-375-empty-fontfix.png` ("They just happen to love this one"), all variants card 2 and the "What game do they play?" answer; ads d5, d8, d10 and the block world art on d1.
The page promises my kid's game, then names a different one in small print in a card halfway down. For a Roblox or Fortnite family this reads as a bait and switch, and it is the moment I lose trust. It also invites the search that turns up League's Teen rating and toxicity reputation, which the page never addresses.
What would fix it for me: be upfront and early. Say it is League of Legends, say in one line why (a team game that needs five kids to talk and plan), and say what it means if my kid plays something else (beginners welcome, the coach teaches the game). Stop saying "the game they already love" anywhere League is the answer.

### PX-3 MAJOR: Nothing about safety, strangers or supervision
Screenshots: all landing variants (the FAQ), `popup-375-empty.png`, `popup-1440-empty.png`.
My first question about anything online with my kid is who else is in the room. The page never says the team is private, never says there are no strangers or public matches, never says chat or voice is supervised, never says sessions are watched or recorded. "Who are the coaches? Experienced players who are trained to coach kids" sounds like "gamers", not "vetted adults". "Coached online from home" plus a game with a toxicity reputation plus no safety words equals me hesitating.
What would fix it for me: a few concrete facts, not slogans: private team of the same kids, coach present the whole session, background checked coaches, sessions recorded, no public lobbies or strangers. Put one line of it where I see it before the email box, and the rest in the coaches answer.

### PX-4 MAJOR: Nobody I can see is behind this
Screenshots: all landing variants (footer "EKUZO · Youth esports with real coaches"), `landing-d3-1440-empty-fontfix.png`.
"Season One" tells me it is brand new. There is no parent quote, no "since 2023", no "X kids coached", no name or face of anyone running it, no About link, no privacy link near the form. I have no way to tell whether this is a real program or a startup collecting emails. The d3 photo has "Boys & Girls of Boston" on the jerseys, which would actually reassure me if anyone told me what it was.
What would fix it for me: one line of proof (how long they have been coaching kids, one real parent sentence) and a visible link to who they are and the privacy policy.

### PX-5 MINOR: "What does it cost?" is never answered
Screenshots: all variants, "Do I pay anything now?" answer.
"No, not now" is honest but makes me assume it is expensive later. For a free list this does not stop me signing up, but it means the first price I see later could surprise me.
What would fix it for me: a rough idea ("about the cost of a rec sports season") or "we'll share pricing before anyone pays".

### PX-6 MINOR: After sign-up I do not know when I will hear from them
Screenshots: `landing-d3-375-confirm.png`, `landing-d3-1440-confirm.png`, `popup-375-confirm.png`.
"We'll be in touch" and "2027" leave me guessing: tomorrow, or next summer? Is there a confirmation email now? Who is it from?
What would fix it for me: "Check your inbox for a note from EKUZO in the next few minutes. After that we'll write about once a month until sign-ups open in [month] 2027."

### PX-7 MINOR: The "what do they play?" question contradicts the page
Screenshots: `landing-d3-375-confirm.png`, `landing-d3-1440-confirm.png`, `popup-375-confirm.png`.
Right after reading "They play League of Legends", I am asked if my kid plays Fortnite, Roblox, Minecraft. I wonder: is there a Fortnite team coming? If I tap Roblox, does my kid get turned away? Am I being sorted?
What would fix it for me: one sentence of why they ask ("Season one is League of Legends. Tell us what they play so we can help them get started" or whatever is true).

### PX-8 MINOR: "2027" feels far away, and "Save their spot" overpromises
Screenshots: `landing-d3-375-empty-fontfix.png`, `landing-d3-1440-empty-fontfix.png` (hero "Season one · 2027"), all variants ("Spots for season one are limited", "Save their spot" next to "doesn't commit you to anything").
My problem is tonight's argument about logging off. "2027" with no month could mean 3 months or 11. And the button says I am saving a spot while the fine print says I am not signed up for anything; "spots are limited" on a free list for next year reads as fake urgency.
What would fix it for me: name the month or season ("winter 2027"), and either say what the list really gets me (first access to sign-ups) or soften "save their spot".

### PX-9 MINOR: What equipment and what schedule?
Screenshots: all variants, "How a season works" and FAQ.
"Weekly practice" but which evening and how long? And what does my kid need? We have a Switch and a school Chromebook. If this needs a real computer I want to know now, not after I get excited.
What would fix it for me: one line each: "Weekly, about 90 minutes on a weekday evening" and "needs a computer (PC or Mac) and a headset".

### PX-10 NIT: Confirmation tone may not land with a worried parent
Screenshots: `landing-d3-375-confirm.png`, `popup-375-confirm.png`.
"We can't wait to make screentime the highlight of your day too" lands oddly after an ad that started with "they game too much". "Achievement unlocked: Parent of the year +100 XP" is cute and made me smile, but a parent who signed up out of worry may feel teased. "Now go outside" is fine.
What would fix it for me: keep the playfulness, but make the first line about my kid ("We'll help make their screen time something you're proud of").

### PX-11 NIT: The page still asks for my email after I signed up
Screenshots: `landing-d3-375-confirm.png`, `landing-d3-1440-confirm.png`.
Scrolling down after "You're on the list" I see "Get them on the list" with an empty email box. It made me wonder if the first one went through. On the phone the EKUZO logo also disappears at the top of the confirmation.
What would fix it for me: once I am on the list, the lower box says so.

### PX-12 NIT: d4 page gives me the least to go on
Screenshot: `landing-d4-375-empty-fontfix.png`.
d1 has the "cross it off" cards and d10 has the skill cards; d4 goes straight from one paragraph to the generic steps. As a d4 parent I get the least reason to trust or care.
What would fix it for me: a short block like d10's that says what being on a team does for a kid.

### Checked, not findings
- The fixed "Save their spot" bar overlapping the section heading mid-page in the 375 captures is the capture artifact noted in the brief; I did not count it.
- The email form is short (email only) and the "For parents. We only email about EKUZO" line is reassuring. Keep it.
- Desktop layout (`landing-d3-1440-empty-fontfix.png`) reads well: ad on the left, ask on the right. The empty space under the hero is fine.
