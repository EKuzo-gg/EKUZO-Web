# My kid doesn't like sports. What now?

*What a team gives a kid, and where to look for one.*

**Short answer:** nothing is wrong with your kid. Fewer than half of kids play a team sport on a
regular basis. What's worth replacing is what a good team gives them: the same people every week, a
coach who knows them, a season that builds to something, and a job other kids count on them to do.
Before you sign them up for the next thing, look at what they already love, and find a team built
around that. For a lot of kids, that's video games.

## Is something wrong if my son or daughter doesn't like sports?

No. In the Aspen Institute's [State of Play 2025](https://projectplay.org/state-of-play-2025/participation-trends)
report, no more than 42% of boys ages 6 to 17 have played a team sport regularly in any of the
last nine years. For girls it's 37%. Your kid has plenty of company.

So the worry is usually about something else. An educator said it well in
[a video we made about our program](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pyicgs8RXnc&t=90s):

> "It's really important to be in an environment where you're working on a team, you're seeing
> what you're made of... and so if your kid's not into sports, how are they going to get that?"

I was that kid. Here's how it went for me.

## I was the kid who didn't like sports

I grew up in Sugar Land, Texas, in a family of doctors. School ran from 7 to 3. After that came
piano, cello, swim team and whatever else was on the calendar until 8, then homework, then bed. In
high school I was on the tennis team. It was not good.

None of it took. I wasn't into sports and I wasn't into fine arts. I had a lot of social anxiety,
and I didn't feel connected to much of anything.

The one thing I loved was the thing my parents liked least. When I was 15, my older brother and
four of his friends would come over after school and play League of Legends in our living room,
all on one team, laughing and yelling. I wanted to feel that.

I didn't get it until college, when I joined the League of Legends team at UT Dallas. We had team
dinners. I learned who at home was rooting for each of my teammates. When we won our region, my
mom called on video and showed me the room: my family, watching our match on the big TV.

Later that spring I was home on the couch, rewatching "Inception," when my mom slapped me on the
back of the head and asked why I was watching a silly movie when I should be practicing. I went to
my room, queued up a game and cried. For the first time, I felt like my mom saw me.
[The Athletic opened a story with that moment](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/5069385/2023/11/17/esports-league-of-legends-college-gaming/)
in 2023.

I was 21. I started EKUZO so a kid could have that at 10, with a coach.

## What should I look at before I sign my kid up for the next thing?

Everything my parents signed me up for was a fair guess. The thing I loved was in our living room
the whole time.

So before the next sign-up, watch for a week. What does your kid do when no one is making them?
What do they talk about until you stop following? Who are they with when they do it?

For some kids that's drawing, taking things apart or the drums. Then stage crew, robotics or band
could be their team. For a lot of kids it's a video game. You may not love what you find. My
parents didn't. Start there anyway.

> **Try this tonight**
> 1. Ask what they liked and didn't like about the last team or class they were on. Listen for
>    whether it was the activity, the coach or the other kids.
> 2. Ask: "If you could be on a team for anything, what would it be?"
> 3. Sit in on one game they play and count the people they're playing with. There may already be
>    a team in there.

## What does a team give a kid?

Once you know what they love, look for four things around it.

1. **The same people every week.** That's how kids make real friends.
2. **A coach.** An adult who knows your kid and tells them the truth kindly.
3. **A season that builds to something.** Opening night, a concert, a tournament. A date on the
   calendar gives practice a point.
4. **A job other kids count on.** I learned you can only build something special with a team.

## Could a video game team be your kid's team?

If your kid plays every day and has never clicked with a sport, it's worth a look. A parent in
[another of our videos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVkSj6AYCDs) described it this way:

> "We're not a super athletic family, so we don't have baseball or soccer or those sorts of things
> to go to, but EKUZO in a huge way sort of fills that same sort of role."

Colleges came around to it while I was still playing. After our season, the athletics director at
UT Dallas emailed me. The school made us varsity athletes, with jerseys, trainers and
scholarships. The game stayed the same. The adults around it changed.

[Season 01, our coached team season](/season1/join), puts kids 9 and older on teams of five with
the same coach and the same teammates all season. They practice online twice a week, and the season
builds to a tournament in the spring. They play League of Legends, and most kids start from
scratch.

A gaming team doesn't replace moving. We teach our players that the body and the mind are
connected: to play your best, you have to be at your best. So our practices include pushups and
water, and kids still need to get outside.

If your kid's friends are mostly online, read [when your son's only friends are online](/blog/when-your-sons-only-friends-are-online).

## Common questions

**My son doesn't like sports. Is that a problem?**
No. Most boys don't play a team sport regularly, and that's been true for years. Look for a team
built around something he does care about.

**What if my daughter doesn't like sports?**
Same answer. Fewer girls than boys play a team sport regularly, so she has company too. Look for
the same four things: the same people every week, a coach, a season and a role.

**Should I make my kid play a sport?**
That's your call, and plenty of families have a "one activity a season" rule. If you have one, let
your kid choose the activity.

**What activities are good for a kid who isn't athletic?**
Start with what they already love. A kid who draws might like stage crew. A kid who takes things
apart might like robotics. A kid who plays video games every day might like a coached team.

**My kid only wants to play video games. Does that count as an activity?**
Playing alone in a bedroom is a hobby. The same game on a team, with a coach, a schedule and a
season, is an activity.
