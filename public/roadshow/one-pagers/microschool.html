For Austin microschools

Your kids already game.
Give them a team.

One coached team inside your school's cycle, led by college esports players.

“They're not really part of a team, and they don't have a way to do that.”

An Austin microschool founder, October 2026

League of Legends is five on five, so a team needs ten players. Spread across grades, a school of 20 or 30 rarely has ten kids who can play together. We pool them into one team and give them a coach.

One session, about 90 minutes

Warm-up, drill
15 min

In game as a team
About 45 min

Review with the coach
30 min

Once or twice a week, in your school day or after it. Pushups and water between games.

EKUZO brings

  • A college coach for every 10 kids
  • A plan for each block
  • Review after every session
  • A parent explainer and weekly notes
  • A family showcase to close the block
  • A write-up of each player's progress

Your school brings

  • 10 to 12 students, ages 9 to 17
  • A time slot in your week
  • A room with computers, or kids at home
  • One staff contact
  • A note to your families

Your cycle

A 6-week block, or two in a row.

Your outcomes

Goals for each player, and a coach's write-up at the end.

Credit, if you want it

Your school leads. We share each week's work so you can map it.

First step: Karlin meets your students

Our founder logs in with your students for 30 minutes to share his story and take their questions. Then we shape the first block with you, at a founding-school rate for Austin's first partner schools.

Karlin Oei founded EKUZO. He was the anxious kid who found his people through games, and college esports helped pay for his school.

All EKUZO coaches are top 1% collegiate esports athletes or former professional players.
