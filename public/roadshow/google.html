Google Search

Parents aren't searching for youth esports. They're searching for help with a kid who games too much, doesn't have friends, or doesn't fit the soccer-and-scouts mold. Each campaign meets one of those problems in the words parents actually type.

The answer is the same every time: a coach and a team that has their back, in whatever game they already love. You get to be proud of them. They get a place where they feel seen. Every ad lands on the waitlist page the Meta ads use, opened on the matching message.

What parents see

Every ad, on a phone

One example per ad group, at the top of Google on mobile. Google mixes the headlines, so each is one of many combinations. Tap through to the landing page it opens on.

Account structure

One campaign per problem

Each problem gets its own budget, so the cheapest one to win can be fed more and the others don't starve it. Esports searches get a small catch budget only. Search charges only when someone searches, so if nobody does, it costs nothing.

          Settings

          How it spends

          NetworksGoogle Search only. Turn off Search Partners and the Display Network. Both are on by default and both burn budget on low-intent clicks.
          LocationUnited States, set to "Presence: people in or regularly in". The default "presence or interest" pays for searches from outside the US.
          Budget$16/day, about $490 a month: $6 screen time, $4 friends, $4 not a sports kid, $2 catch. Same total as the Meta Tier 1 test, so the two read side by side.
          BiddingWeeks 1 to 2: Maximize clicks with a CPC cap ($1.50 on the problem campaigns, $2.50 on the catch). Once the account has 15 sign-ups in 30 days, switch to Maximize conversions. At 30, set a target cost per sign-up.
          AudiencesAdd Parents of Teens (13 to 17) and Parents of Grade-Schoolers (6 to 12) as observation, not targeting. You see how parents convert without shrinking reach. Google gives no personalized ads to anyone under 18, so we never target kids.
          Final URL suffix
          Auto-taggingOn. It adds a gclid to every click, which the landing page should store with the email.
          Negative keywords · both campaigns

          Searches we never pay for

          Jobs, scholarships, downloads and cheats, gear, screen-time apps and settings, mental health and diagnosis searches, bullying, and anyone looking to learn a specific game. We don't teach the game, so we don't pay for those searches. Check the search terms report every Monday and add to this list. That one habit does more for cost per email than any copy change.

          Reading it

          What to watch, weekly

          1. Cost per waitlist email, by ad group, next to Meta's cost per Lead for the same week. The proposed signal target was $25 or less per email.
          2. Search terms. Anything irrelevant becomes a negative. Any term that converts twice becomes its own exact keyword.
          3. Kill rule. Pause a keyword once it has spent $50 with no sign-up. After 3 weeks, compare cost per email across the three problems. Move budget toward the cheapest one. That answer, which problem parents most want solved, is worth as much as the emails.
          4. Gamer vs player. Filter ads by the TERM_GAMER and TERM_PLAYER labels. Treat it as directional until each side has about 20 sign-ups.
          5. Feed Meta. The headlines Google rates Best are tested parent language. Move them into the Ad Lab primary text.
