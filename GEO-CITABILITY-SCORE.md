### 2. "Why games work as learning systems?" intro — Score: 62/100

**Current opening:**
> "Every great game is a learning environment. Players stay engaged because they're motivated, supported, and challenged just enough to grow — the same best practices that drive effective classrooms."

**Problem:** Decent opener but generic — could describe any gaming program. No statistics, no named research, no EKUZO-specific claim.

**Suggested rewrite:**
> "Games work as learning systems because they apply four evidence-based principles from educational psychology: intrinsic motivation, calibrated challenge (what Csikszentmihalyi called 'flow'), immediate feedback loops, and social scaffolding. **Additional improvements:**
- Cite the flow-state research (Csikszentmihalyi) or a specific education study by name

## Summary for Jamie

The `/methodology` page is EKUZO's most important content-marketing asset — it's where the whole brand story lives — but right now it's optimized for human skim-reading, not AI extraction. The structure (H1/H2/H3, ordered list, founder quote) is solid; the problem is **content density**. Every section is a headline with no body.

The single highest-leverage move is expanding the ten pillars from one-liners into short, self-contained explanatory blocks (~60 words each) with at least one named research source and one concrete EKUZO example per pillar. That alone would take this page from ~45 to ~75.

Second priority: add program outcome statistics. "68% of Teams participants had never played a school sport" or "Average attendance rate across EKUZO programs is X%" — whatever the real numbers are. AI systems weight statistical density heavily, and the page currently has zero numbers outside "ten pillars."
